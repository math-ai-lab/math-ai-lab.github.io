How much can we learn about a network from a handful of distances? This question connects the lab’s work in graph theory with its investigation of mathematical reasoning in AI.

Ongoing investigation

When does a language model mistake local evidence for a global proof?

Two neighboring stations can have different distances from a sensor even when two distant stations give identical readings. We are studying whether language models recognize this distinction.

Explore a three-station counterexample

Take the path A–B–C and place one sensor at B. The readings are A = 1, B = 0, C = 1. Every neighboring pair has different readings, yet A and C are indistinguishable. The sensor resolves adjacent pairs only.

The planned evaluation compares direct answers with answers prompted to look for a counterexample. It also tests valid claims and repeats questions with renamed vertices.
